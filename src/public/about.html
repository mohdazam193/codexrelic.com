From Frontline Support to Enterprise Reliability: The Story So Far

Hi, I’m Mohd Azam. Today, I work as a DevOps Engineer II & Site Reliability Engineer at Mitratech in Hyderabad, safeguarding multi-tenant cloud platforms for Global Fortune 500 legal and compliance enterprises.

My path to DevOps wasn’t a straight, paved highway. It is a story of starting from scratch after graduating college in 2014, working the overnight trenches of technical support and real-time operations, and relentlessly teaching myself systems, clouds, and automation to build a career in production reliability. Seeing software from the user and support perspective first changed how I build systems forever.

The Crucible & Evolution

1. Starting from Scratch: Electronic Arts & The Frontline

2013 – 2014

After finishing my engineering degree, there were no ready-made cloud infrastructure jobs waiting for me. I started at ground zero in global technical support at iEnergizer for Electronic Arts (EA Worldwide Customer Experience).

When blockbuster titles like Battlefield 4, FIFA 14, and Titanfall launched to millions of players worldwide, I was on the frontlines resolving game crashes, TCP/UDP packet drops, NAT traversal errors, and server connection failures. It was a baptism by fire. I saw firsthand what happens when distributed systems buckle under unexpected load, and how critical empathy, clear communication, and rapid root-cause diagnosis are when users are hurting.

2. Amazon Global Command Center: The Pulse of Scale

2016 – 2019

Moving to Amazon, I stepped into the Global Command Center as a Real-Time Analyst. Here, scale wasn't an abstract buzzword—it was real-time telemetry from four international marketplaces pulsating across operational walls.

During high-stakes events like Prime Day and Black Friday, I monitored live queue health, handled real-time incident routing, and authored process wikis (GRASP) to standardize triage across cross-functional teams. This experience instilled in me an obsession with telemetry, real-time observability, and eliminating operational bottlenecks.

3. The Cloud & SRE Evolution: Mitratech

2020 – 2026

With years of operational intuition under my belt, I transitioned into core enterprise systems engineering at Mitratech. Over 5+ years, I grew from Application Administrator to leading cloud platform operations for mission-critical SaaS suites (TeamConnect, TAP, Policy Hub, CMO, INSZoom) across Azure, AWS, Oracle Cloud, and Rackspace.

I built comprehensive New Relic and AppDynamics observability fabrics, led full datacenter migrations (Rackspace → Azure), and maintained strict 99.9%+ uptime SLOs. Driven by the memory of manual support pain, I waged war on repetitive toil: writing automated cleanup scripts, cutting deployment windows from 50 minutes down to 10 minutes, and saving $4,300+/year in cloud waste through proactive FinOps.

4. Today: DevSecOps, Platform Engineering & Beyond

Present

Promoted to DevOps Engineer II, I now focus on shifting security left and building self-healing infrastructure. From codifying immutable environments in Terraform to enforcing SAST/DAST security gates in Azure DevOps and experimenting with Kubernetes-native toolsets (Falco, Trivy, GitOps), I bridge the gap between development speed and ironclad production stability.

Why the Support Background Matters

Engineers who start in pure development often see production as abstract code running on abstract servers. Starting from support teaches you the human side of downtime:

01 • USER EMPATHY Downtime is Personal

A 500 error isn't just an HTTP status code—it’s an end-user blocked from finishing critical legal work or an online transaction.

02 • ACTIONABLE TELEMETRY Observability for Humans

Having triaged frantic alerts, I design dashboards and runbooks that on-call responders can actually understand at 3:00 AM.

03 • CONTINUOUS LEARNING Always a Practitioner

Starting from zero taught me how to learn anything from first principles—whether reverse-engineering X.509 certs or configuring k8s networking.

Core Philosophies
01 / RELIABILITY

SLOs Over Hype

Reliability is a feature. Measurable error budgets and proactive observability give development teams the safety to move fast without breaking production.

02 / AUTOMATION

Ruthless Toil Reduction

If a manual task has to be performed more than twice, it deserves a script, a pipeline, or an architectural fix.

03 / SECURITY

Shift-Left & Zero-Trust

Security isn't a post-deployment checklist. Embedding SAST/DAST, secret scanning, and least-privilege IAM directly into pipelines prevents disasters early.

What I’m Building: devops-cms
OPEN SOURCE • IN ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT

devops-cms — A Self-Reliant Portfolio Engine for SRE & Platform Engineers

GIT CLONE & DEPLOY

Most developer portfolio templates out there are built for frontend UI developers. They look flashy, but they don't showcase what a systems engineer or SRE actually cares about—observability widgets, telemetry streams, infrastructure as code, zero-trust headers, real uptime metrics, and automated GitOps publishing.

That’s why I’m putting my nights and weekends into building devops-cms. It’s an entirely self-reliant, production-grade portfolio platform designed exclusively for DevOps, SRE, and Platform Engineers. Anyone—from a junior trying to land their first cloud gig to a seasoned principal architect—can simply clone the repository, drop in their data or LaTeX resume, and have a rock-solid, secure, and blazing-fast site running with automated CI/CD.

More than just a website, it’s a living playground. It lets engineers experiment with production patterns: real-time WebSocket telemetry (like the live VM widget on this page), automated security scanning (Trivy, SAST), hardened CSP headers, and container orchestration with zero bloated frameworks.
